# Front carrier proposal

`imports/mechanical/nema11-front-carrier.step` is original project CAD in board
coordinates, generated by `scripts/build_motor_carrier.py`. It is a proposed
6061-T6 aluminum part, not a supplier model or a released machining drawing.
The original OEM motor files are unchanged.

The 1 mm front plate uses four 3.4 mm clearance holes on the documented 23 mm
motor front pattern. A 23.4 mm central clearance opening accommodates both the
drawing's 22 mm pilot and the shallow wider front collar present in the OEM STEP.
It is not a precision motor locator. Two side walls extend outside the maximum
28.3 mm frame, leaving 1.35 mm nominal clearance on each side. Both X sides stay
open for the motor leads; the motor is rotated so its fixed leads exit positive X
toward the JST edge. No rear holes or rear shaft are assumed.

Four rear bosses support the PCB at its separate 26 mm mounting pattern. The
model represents 2.05 mm tap drills; these require **M2.5 × 0.45 through threads**,
which are not represented by modeled helices. The boss support plane is Z=-0.8
mm, the PCB bottom. The motor front face remains Z=-42 mm.

The nominal solid audit checks the carrier against the unchanged OEM motor,
PCB and all supplier parts whose bounds can reach it. No solid interference was
found. This does not establish tolerance fit, vibration strength, thermal behavior
or physical fastener/tool access. Separate conditional envelope checks pass
under the purchasing limits below.

The candidate screw lengths are four M2.5×4 at the motor front and four M2.5×5
at the PCB. Procurement requires actual supplier drawings and tolerances. With
a 1.0±0.1 mm front plate and 3.7–4.1 mm screw length, front engagement would be
2.6–3.2 mm, below the motor's 3.5 mm minimum tapped depth; these are proposed
procurement limits, not verified purchased screw specifications. At nominal PCB
thickness, a 5 mm PCB screw engages 3.4 mm. Verify maximum head diameter, height,
hex access, length tolerance, thread runout, tightening torque and retention
before release. The machine-side mounting interface remains unspecified.

A released drawing must specify machining tolerances, flatness, deburring,
surface finish and thread inspection, and verify pilot projection and shaft
clearance over their drawing tolerances. The full harness and mating connector
also require separate assembly verification.
